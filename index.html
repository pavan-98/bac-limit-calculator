Questions

What is the legal alcohol limit for driving in India?

30 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood, which is 0.03% BAC. It's set by Section 185 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, and police check it with a breath analyser. That's stricter than the 0.08% limit in many other countries. A first offence can mean a fine of up to ₹10,000, up to 6 months in jail, or both, and your licence can be suspended.

What do the numbers on a police breathalyser mean?

Breathalysers used by police in India show the alcohol in your blood in mg per 100 ml. The legal limit is 30. To turn a reading into BAC %, divide it by 1,000: 30 is 0.03%, and 205 is 0.205%, almost 7 times the limit. On average the number drops by about 15 every hour, so a reading of 205 takes around 12 hours to get under 30.

How many drinks can I have and still drive?

Very few, often none. By this calculator's estimate, a 70 kg man is over 0.03% straight after one large peg (60 ml of whisky) or one 650 ml bottle of beer, and a 55 kg woman is over after a single 330 ml beer. The only number that's always safe is zero.

How long does it take to get under the limit?

On average your body clears about 0.015% BAC per hour, and nothing speeds that up: not coffee, a cold shower or eating after you drink. So at 0.06% (twice the limit) it takes about 2 hours to get under 0.03%, and about 4 hours for the alcohol to be gone.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's an estimate. It uses the Widmark formula with an average burn-off rate. Your real level can be higher because of food, medicines, your health, your body and how fast you drank. Treat the time it shows as a rough guide, not a guarantee, and when in doubt, don't drive.
